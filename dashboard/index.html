Overview

Definitions
Critical equipment
Machines whose most recent inspection carries a Critical (grade X) finding. Grade decides severity: A normal, B incipient, C degraded, X critical.
Degraded equipment
Machines whose most recent inspection carries a Degraded (grade C) finding and nothing worse.
Overdue inspections
Rounds past their interval, counted in machine hours at 20 h/day, on the Type and Class filters above.
Open maintenance actions
Findings that need work and are not closed — including those not yet planned.
Unassigned actions
Open maintenance actions with no responsible person.
Grade review required
Findings whose recorded severity does not match their grade. Review the grade; severity follows it.
Inspection compliance
Rounds walked before they were overdue, of every round that came due; and machines each round applies to, against machines it reached, in the period selected above.
Condition trend
Findings by month, worst on top. One month is a sample; three is a trend.
Equipment requiring attention
Worst finding on each machine's most recent inspection, highest priority first. Click a machine to open its history.

P-F interval — detection to functional failure

How this is counted
Measured wear against its limit. P is where the part becomes a finding (80% worn); F is condemn. The gap between them is the warning the round buys you — if it is shorter than the inspection interval, the round will miss it.

ISO 14224 failure mechanism

Table B.2 mechanism classes — comparable across sites and benchmarks.

Affected equipment

Wear and remaining life

How this is counted
Every measured position, latest reading first. Life is projected from this position's own history — two readings at least, and the app says so when it will not guess.

Action register

How this is counted
Every finding that needs work, worst first. Give it an owner and a date, and it stops being a list and starts being a plan.

Inspection history

Every round, against the clock it is scheduled on

How this is counted
Hours, not days — a machine parked for three weeks has not worn. Each row is one unit and one round, counted from the last one recorded anywhere in the system and at the machine's own operating rate where two hour-meter readings have measured it. A round somebody chose not to walk says so, with the reason they gave.

Programme coverage

Methodology
How much of the fleet is audited, verified and standardised — against the register, not against the machines somebody happened to visit. Every number states its denominator, because those two get mistaken for each other and that is how a programme reports 90% and means 90% of eleven machines.

Deciding the standard

How this is counted
Not product by product — requirement by requirement. Machines that need the same thing are one decision, however many badges are on them. Choose, and the matrix and the posters re-read.

The 2027 shelf against the recommendations

How this is counted
Specification 02 rev CO-05 is what the site may actually buy. The fleet matrices say what should go in each compartment. Where the two do not line up, somebody has either told a fitter to use an oil nobody ordered, or ordered an oil for nothing — and the season is a third answer neither of them can show.

Machine reference

How this is counted
Capacities, specifications and intervals, per model. A capacity is the one number here that causes a failure on its own — an under-filled final drive seizes, an over-filled one blows a seal — so changing it asks where the figure came from.

Fleet matrix

How this is counted
Every asset class on one sheet — compartment, capacity, interval, and the oil that belongs in it. Colour is identity and nothing else: which product, never how good it is.

Not on the sheet

How this is counted
The matrix can only show a machine where the register's spelling of its model and the reference's spelling of its model meet. Where they miss each other, the machine has no lubrication standard and nothing above says so. Both halves are listed here: nothing is dropped quietly.
Machines with no reference
References reaching no machine
A reference with no machine is not always a fault: several are for equipment specified or on order but not yet on site. It is listed so the reference and the fleet can be read against each other, not as a defect list.

Shop posters

How this is counted
The sheet that goes on the workshop wall. A standard that lives only in a database is not a standard — this is the version a fitter reads at arm's length with a head-torch, so it is bilingual, it is large, and it says what to do when the right drum is empty.

Severities nobody can check

    How this is counted
    A severity is not a field somebody types here — it is read off the grade (A→Normal, B→Incipient, C→Degraded, X→Critical), or off the millimetres on an undercarriage round. These are the places a record carries a severity that neither of those produces, so the board is showing a finding no one can re-derive. The fix is always the GRADE: set it and the severity follows. Rounds imported from the original spreadsheets are where nearly all of these come from.

    Records waiting on evidence

    How this is counted
    Every photograph and video a record says it has, checked against the file names the server actually holds. A record is waiting when one of its own attachments has not arrived.

    Backend

    Where the number comes from

    How this is counted
    Read down. Each line is a source or a rule, and the last one is the figure every other screen counts. The folder is the only source of rounds — the sixteen imported from the original spreadsheets were written into it on 29 August 2026 and are no longer counted separately. A round two phones both sent is one round with a conflict to settle, not two rounds.

    Where the photographs are

    Photographs bundled with this app live beside it, never in the sync folder. They are not missing field uploads and are counted separately.

    Admin diagnostics

      Not measurable from here

        How this is counted
        These need a backend that issues a durable receipt per record and a hash per attachment. This one stores files in folders, so the dashboard cannot prove delivery latency, detect a corrupted upload or count a suppressed duplicate. They are listed rather than reported as zero.

        Records that cannot be trusted as they stand

        How this is counted
        A record missing the identity it needs to be filed — unit, date or round — or carrying an inspection point nobody has identified. Nothing is deleted; the inspection is on hold here so it is visible rather than silently dropped from every count.
